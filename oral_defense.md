# Oral defense

Use the short answer first. Give the technical backup only if the professor
asks for more detail.

## 1. Economic motivation

### Basic -- What is the paper's central question?

**Short answer.** Can agentic AI improve today's personalized decisions while
crowding out the human effort that reproduces collective knowledge for future
cohorts?

**Technical backup.** AI raises private idiosyncratic precision \(Y\), which
reduces the best response \(e(X,\tau_A)\). Lower aggregate effort weakens the
public signal and therefore future \(X\).

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not say the paper assumes AI directly destroys
knowledge; the channel operates through endogenous human effort.

### Intermediate -- What is knowledge collapse?

**Short answer.** It is convergence of public precision about the common state
to zero, together with zero human effort in the baseline.

**Technical backup.** Under \(\Delta_I=0\), \(X=0\) implies \(e=0\), so
\(F(0)=0\). In some parameter regions that fixed point is stable or globally
attracting.

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not equate collapse with AI making current
predictions uniformly worse.

### Difficult -- Why do short-lived agents matter?

**Short answer.** They value their current decision but not the public
knowledge their effort leaves to later cohorts.

**Technical backup.** An atomistic agent takes \(X\) and the future law of
motion as given. The private FOC contains \(\lambda_I\) but not \(\lambda_G\),
although aggregate effort later enters public precision through \(\lambda_GE\).

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not claim agents are irrational; the missing
incentive is an externality.

## 2. Information and precision

### Basic -- What do \(X\) and \(Y\) mean?

**Short answer.** \(X\) is public precision about the state common to all
cases; \(Y\) is an individual's posterior precision about the particular case.

**Technical backup.** Precision is inverse variance. The public history is
summarized by \(X\), while \(Y=\sigma^{-2}+\lambda_Ie+\tau_A\).

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not call \(X\) an action or \(Y\) a payoff.

### Intermediate -- Why do the terms in \(Y\) add?

**Short answer.** Independent Gaussian sources add precision when Bayes' rule
combines them.

**Technical backup.** Multiplying the prior and independent normal likelihoods
adds their quadratic log-density coefficients, giving posterior precision
\(\sigma^{-2}+\lambda_Ie+\tau_A\).

**Common mistake to avoid.** Variances do not add in this updating formula;
precisions do.

### Difficult -- Why is \(G(\tau)=2\Phi(\sqrt\tau)-1\)?

**Short answer.** It is the probability that a normal posterior error falls
inside the model's unit tolerance band.

**Technical backup.** If \(Z\sim N(0,\tau^{-1})\), then
\(\Pr(|Z|\leq1)=\Pr(|\sqrt\tau Z|\leq\sqrt\tau)
=2\Phi(\sqrt\tau)-1\).

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not describe \(G\) as precision itself; it is
a probability generated by precision.

## 3. Agent's problem

### Basic -- What does the agent choose and take as given?

**Short answer.** The agent chooses effort \(e\geq0\) and takes inherited
public precision \(X\) and AI precision \(\tau_A\) as given.

**Technical backup.** Effort is chosen before signal realizations. Predictions
are posterior means after the signals arrive.

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not say the agent chooses \(X\) or \(Y\)
directly.

### Intermediate -- Why are probabilities multiplied by payoffs?

**Short answer.** Expected production weights each payoff increment by the
probability of the event that earns it.

**Technical backup.** \(G(X)\Delta_G\) weights the stand-alone common gain;
\(G(X)G(Y)\Delta_X\) weights the complementarity gain by joint correctness.

**Common mistake to avoid.** \(\Delta_G\) and \(\Delta_X\) are payoff
increments, not probabilities.

### Difficult -- Why are \(G(X)\) and \(G(Y)\) multiplied?

**Short answer.** The complementarity payoff requires both predictions to be
correct, and the common and idiosyncratic posterior errors are independent.

**Technical backup.** Independence implies
\(\Pr(C\cap I)=\Pr(C)\Pr(I)=G(X)G(Y)\).

**Common mistake to avoid.** The product would need reconsideration if the
states or signal errors were correlated.

## 4. First-order condition

### Basic -- What does the FOC say economically?

**Short answer.** The agent increases effort until its marginal expected
production gain equals marginal effort cost.

**Technical backup.** For \(X>0\),
\(\lambda_I\Delta_XG(X)g(Y)=e^{1/\varepsilon}\).

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not use this equality as an interior FOC at
\(X=0\); the constrained solution there is a corner.

### Intermediate -- What does each factor in marginal benefit do?

**Short answer.** \(\lambda_I\) converts effort into precision, \(g(Y)\)
converts precision into extra success probability, \(G(X)\) is the chance the
common input is correct, and \(\Delta_X\) is the payoff unlocked by joint
success.

**Technical backup.** They appear by the chain rule applied to
\(G(X)G(Y)\Delta_X\).

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not omit \(Y_e=\lambda_I\).

### Difficult -- Why is the interior optimum unique for \(X>0\)?

**Short answer.** Marginal benefit is positive at zero and decreases with
effort, while marginal cost starts at zero and strictly increases.

**Technical backup.** Since \(g'(Y)<0\), the payoff part is strictly concave;
the power cost is strictly convex. Endpoint signs give one crossing.

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not rely only on writing the FOC; explain the
curvature and endpoint signs.

## 5. Complements and substitutes

### Basic -- Why does \(X\) complement effort?

**Short answer.** Better common-state knowledge raises the probability that
case-specific learning earns the complementarity payoff.

**Technical backup.** \(U_{eX}=\lambda_I\Delta_Xg(X)g(Y)>0\) for \(X>0\).

**Common mistake to avoid.** The result is about complementarity in incentives,
not a claim that \(X\) and effort enter the same precision.

### Intermediate -- Why does AI substitute for effort?

**Short answer.** AI and effort both raise \(Y\); diminishing returns to
precision make the next human-produced unit less valuable.

**Technical backup.** \(Y_{\tau_A}=1\) and
\(U_{e\tau_A}=\lambda_I\Delta_XG(X)g'(Y)<0\) on the interior.

**Common mistake to avoid.** The crowd-out is not imposed directly in the cost
function.

### Difficult -- What is the difference between a cross-partial and
increasing differences?

**Short answer.** A cross-partial is a local derivative requiring smoothness;
increasing differences compares finite changes and can remain meaningful at a
nonsmooth boundary.

**Technical backup.** \(G(X)\) is nondecreasing on \(X\geq0\), so the
incremental payoff from effort is nondecreasing in \(X\), even though
\(g(0)\) is not finite.

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not infer that a missing finite derivative
destroys the global economic ordering.

## 6. Boundary behavior

### Basic -- What changes at \(X=0\)?

**Short answer.** The baseline complementarity payoff disappears because
\(G(0)=0\), leaving effort with no private benefit.

**Technical backup.** Utility becomes \(f(0,0)-c(e)\), so the unique
constrained optimum is \(e=0\).

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not conclude that utility itself is undefined.

### Intermediate -- Why is \(g(0)\) not finite?

**Short answer.** Near zero, \(g(X)=\phi(\sqrt X)/\sqrt X\) behaves like
\(\phi(0)/\sqrt X\), which diverges.

**Technical backup.** \(G\) is continuous at zero but has no finite right
derivative there.

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not confuse \(G(0)=0\) with \(g(0)=0\).

### Difficult -- Is Observation 1 wrong at the boundary?

**Short answer.** Its economic content survives weakly, but its strict
cross-partial display requires the interior domain \(X>0\).

**Technical backup.** At \(X=0\), \(U_{e\tau_A}=0\) and \(U_{eX}\) is not a
finite classical derivative. The paper separately recognizes the zero-effort
corner.

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not present this as a refutation of the paper's
mechanism.

## 7. Dynamic feedback

### Basic -- How does the static choice affect future knowledge?

**Short answer.** Individual effort aggregates into a public signal, which is
combined with inherited knowledge to determine next period's public precision.

**Technical backup.** The chain is
\(X_t\to e_t\to E_t\to X_{t+1}\), with new public signal precision
\(\lambda_GE_t\).

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not insert \(X_{t+1}\) into the individual's
private FOC.

### Intermediate -- Why is \(\lambda_G\) absent from the FOC?

**Short answer.** It governs the public by-product of effort, which an
atomistic agent does not internalize.

**Technical backup.** \(\lambda_G\) enters
\(X_{t+1}^{-1}=(X_t+\lambda_GE_t)^{-1}+\Sigma^2\), not private utility after
the atomistic external effect is ignored.

**Common mistake to avoid.** Absence from the FOC does not mean
\(\lambda_G\) is technologically irrelevant.

### Difficult -- How can better AI improve today but hurt tomorrow?

**Short answer.** It directly improves private precision today, but it also
reduces effort, public signal production, and inherited precision tomorrow.

**Technical backup.** The direct information effect is positive holding \(X\)
fixed; the indirect steady-state effect through \(e\), \(E\), and future
\(X\) is negative.

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not claim the paper proves every increase in AI
reduces welfare.

## 8. Extension

### Basic -- What assumption does the extension change?

**Short answer.** It changes \(\Delta_I=0\) to \(\Delta_I>0\), so a correct
case-specific prediction has some value even if the common prediction is wrong.

**Technical backup.** The general expected-production decomposition adds
\(G(Y)\Delta_I\).

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not say the extension changes the signal or
aggregation technology.

### Intermediate -- Why does \(\Delta_I>0\) remove the zero fixed point?

**Short answer.** It preserves a private motive for effort at \(X=0\); that
positive effort creates a positive public signal, so zero maps to positive
precision.

**Technical backup.** Strict concavity gives
\(e_\Delta(0,\tau_A)>0\), hence
\(F_\Delta(0)=[\Sigma^2+(\lambda_GIe_\Delta)^{-1}]^{-1}>0\).

**Common mistake to avoid.** Positive boundary effort requires finite AI
precision and a proper finite-variance prior.

### Difficult -- How is this different from Section 5.2?

**Short answer.** Section 5.2 gives public knowledge an exogenous synthetic
signal; our extension makes human effort restart public learning because
case-specific learning remains privately valuable.

**Technical backup.** Both can make the transition positive at zero, but one
changes public data supply and the other changes the production payoff
\(\Delta_I\).

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not claim ours is the only mechanism in the
paper or project that can eliminate the exact zero fixed point.

## 9. Limitations

### Basic -- What exactly does the extension prove?

**Short answer.** It proves a unique positive best response at \(X=0\) and
therefore \(F_\Delta(0)>0\); exact zero is not a steady state.

**Technical backup.** The proof uses endpoint signs and strict concavity of
the modified static objective, then substitutes effort into the public
transition.

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not elevate this own result into a theorem of
the paper.

### Intermediate -- What remains unresolved?

**Short answer.** The number and stability of positive steady states, basins
of attraction, and welfare under the extension.

**Technical backup.** Knowing only \(F_\Delta(0)>0\) does not characterize the
shape or fixed points of the entire transition map.

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not say all low-knowledge equilibria disappear.

### Difficult -- Can welfare fall with better AI in the extension?

**Short answer.** We have not proved either sign.

**Technical backup.** The extension changes both static payoffs and equilibrium
effort, so the paper's baseline welfare propositions cannot be imported
without redoing the dynamic and welfare analysis.

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not answer an open question by analogy alone.

**Common mistake to avoid.** Do not ignore that Section 5.2 reaches a related
